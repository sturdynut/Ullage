What the window holds — chart options

The popover's composition block is one flat bar of four segments plus a two-row legend. The part people open "What's inside" for — which tools filled the window — isn't in the chart at all. Below are five ways to draw the same ContextComposition, each at the popover's real content width (332pt) or the History window's. Hover any mark for its figures.

A. Flat stacked bar today

Four segments in fixed order, shares of what's in the window.

  • Compact: 10pt of bar, ~30pt of legend.
  • Stable — segment order never changes, so the eye learns where "Output" lives.
  • Stops at the first level. Tool results is the one category with real parts, and they are two clicks away in a table.
  • The legend is doing the chart's labelling; it costs two rows because four labels don't fit in one.

B. Treemap recommended

Area = tokens. Categories are tiles; Tool results splits into one tile per tool, Baseline splits off CLAUDE.md when the environment snapshot has it.

  • Shows the second level in the chart itself — "Read is 21k of your window" is visible without expanding anything.
  • Labels go inside tiles, so the two-row legend shrinks to a one-line key. It is about twice A's height (~88pt vs ~42pt) — the cost of showing the parts.
  • Ordered vs squarified is the real decision. Squarified gives nicer aspect ratios but re-sorts by size, so tiles jump positions as the session grows — bad in a live popover. Ordered keeps the fixed category order left→right (the rule in CLAUDE.md) and only stacks tools within their column.
  • Weakness: tiny values vanish. In the real session below, tool results are 3% of the window — a sliver. Hover still works, and that sliver is the honest answer.
  • Estimated tiles keep the ≈, as the legend does today.

C. Two-tier bar (icicle)

Today's bar, plus a thinner row under it that breaks each category into its parts, aligned beneath the parent.

  • The smallest change from today: same reading direction, same colours, +8pt of height.
  • Shows the tool split, but at 6pt tall a part can't carry a label; names live in hover or under the bar for the top two or three.
  • A good fallback if the treemap reads as too busy in the popover.

D. Composition on the window's scale

One bar over the whole window: the segments fill from the left, the empty track is the headroom — the ullage.

  • Merges two bars the popover draws today (occupancy in the header, composition lower down) into one, which answers "how full" and "full of what" together.
  • On a 1M window at 20–30% the segments get thin and the breakdown is hard to read; on a 200k window it's excellent.
  • Better as a replacement for the header bar than for this block — worth a separate decision.

E. Waffle of the window

100 squares, each 1% of the window, filled in fixed category order; empty squares are headroom.

  • Countable — "21 squares used" is concrete in a way a percentage isn't, and the empty squares are a nice fit for the app's name.
  • Quantised to 1%: small categories round to zero squares, so it can't carry tools, and at 1M windows a whole category can be two squares.
  • Pleasant but lossy; it answers the header's question better than this block's.

Recommendation

  1. Replace the flat bar with an ordered treemap (B, "Ordered"). It's the only option that puts the tool breakdown in the chart, it keeps positions stable as the popover updates live, and inside-tile labels let the two-row legend go. Popover: ~88pt tall. History window: ~160pt, where more tools get labels.
  2. Keep the "What's inside" disclosure, but it becomes the table view of the treemap (the full tool list, MCP servers, skills) rather than the only place the parts appear.
  3. Treat D as its own follow-up for the header occupancy bar — it's a good idea, but for a different chart.

Build notes

  • No new data needed. ContextComposition already has tools with per-tool tokens and claudeMdTokensEstimate. The layout (slice-and-dice / squarify) is a pure function of those — it belongs in UllageCore as a tested TreemapLayout struct, and SwiftUI only draws rectangles (a Canvas or a ZStack of positioned views, so each tile gets .help()).
  • The long tail folds into "N more tools" past the top five, rather than generating more shades.
  • Tool tiles are shades of the Tool results teal, so the category still reads by colour; tool identity is by label, not hue.
